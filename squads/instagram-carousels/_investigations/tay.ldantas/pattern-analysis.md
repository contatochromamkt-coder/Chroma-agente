## Language Patterns
### Tone Profile
Reflective, confident, first-person, warm but assertive. Uses rhetorical questions to open ("Vc já teve isso?"), and declarative brand-positioning statements ("ainda seremos a empresa com as melhores soluções de branding e revenue do mundo"). Portuguese (Brazil), informal register ("Vc", emojis like 🤓 and 🤍).

### Hook Patterns
- **Relatable-question hook** (Content 3): "Vc já teve isso? Comparar algo tão bonito que te de vontade de trocar todas as outras coisas..." — opens with a shared experience question before naming a concept (the "efeito Diderot").
- **Bold-claim/manifesto hook** (Content 1): "Construir as marcas mais sexys e ricas dessa geração..." — an aspirational, almost provocative positioning statement used as caption opener.
- **Direct-CTA hook** (Content 2): "A Ruptura será sua maior oportunidade se você se inscrever no link da bio" — names a proper-noun offer/event ("A Ruptura") and pushes straight to the bio link.

### Call-to-Action Patterns
Only 1 of 3 sampled captions has an explicit CTA (Content 2, "link da bio"). The other two rely on engagement through relatability/thought-provoking statements rather than direct asks — comments suggest the audience self-generates engagement (tagging friends, sharing personal stories) without being prompted.

### Vocabulary Signature
Recurring register: "marca(s)", "branding", "revenue", "sexys e ricas" (signature phrase), naming psychological/behavioral concepts by name (e.g., "efeito Diderot") to sound intellectually credible. Mixes Portuguese with English business terms (revenue, branding).

### Style Notes
- Captions are short (1–2 sentences), not long-form educational breakdowns — depth likely lives in the video/carousel visuals rather than the caption text.
- Emoji use is sparse and precise (🤓, 🤍), not emoji-heavy.
- No hashtags observed in any of the 3 sampled captions.

## Recommendations for Squad
1. **Lead carousels and reels with a relatable question, not a headline claim.** The best-performing sampled hook ("Vc já teve isso?") outperforms the manifesto-style opener in comment quality. For Chroma Marketing's educational carousels, open slide 1 with a question the reader has personally experienced before naming the marketing concept being taught.
2. **Name the concept explicitly and give it a "label."** Tay's "efeito Diderot" framing worked because it gave the audience a shareable, credible term. Chroma's carousels should each anchor on one named framework/term (e.g., "o efeito X do marketing") rather than a generic tip list.
3. **Keep captions short; put the teaching inside the visual.** None of the 3 sampled captions exceeded ~2 sentences — the depth was expected to live in the carousel/video, not the caption. Design Chroma's carousel slide copy to carry the substance, and keep the caption as a short frame + optional single CTA.
4. **Use CTAs sparingly and specifically.** Only 1 of 3 posts had a CTA, and it named a specific, branded offer ("A Ruptura") rather than a generic "link in bio." When Chroma includes a CTA, tie it to a named lead magnet/offer, not a vague prompt.
5. **Treat reels as the primary format, carousels as a supporting one.** This profile's current grid skews ~3:1 toward reels over carousels, even though this squad is carousel-focused — recommend Chroma repurpose each carousel's core concept into a short reel script as a companion asset, since reels are what's actually driving this profile's largest engagement numbers.
