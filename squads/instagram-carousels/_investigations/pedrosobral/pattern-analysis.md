# Pattern Analysis: @pedrosobral (Instagram)

Analyzed: 2026-09-02
Sample size: 3 carousel posts (the 3 most recent carousels on the profile; 3 recent reels were seen in the grid but not opened for this pass)

## Executive Summary
Pedro Sobral (2.3M followers, digital-marketing/traffic-ads educator) runs carousels as long-form "argument-building" sequences of 6-8 slides, almost always minimal text-on-plain-background graphics (memes/screenshot mockups, not branded slide templates). Every carousel opens with a punchy, curiosity-driven title slide and either closes with a direct follow-CTA slide or lets the caption carry the CTA. Content splits between (a) reacting to a current-events/market story and drawing a marketing lesson from it, and (b) listicle/numbered-list formats ("N coisas," "N frases") teaching a sales or mindset lesson. Captions are short, conversational, second-person, and frequently end with a question to drive comments.

### Format Structures
Two recurring carousel templates observed:
1. **News-jacking / storytelling carousel** (Content 1): Hook slide states a surprising fact → 2-4 slides narrate what happened chronologically → pivot slide draws the marketing lesson → CTA/follow slide.
2. **Numbered-listicle carousel** (Content 2 and 3): Title slide states the list premise with a number ("5 coisas," "8 frases") → each subsequent slide delivers one list item, often with a short line of context/contrast ("Parece bobagem. Não é.") → not all items always get their own slide alone; some slides also embed a mockup screenshot (bank balance) or a short muted video clip instead of pure text.

### Posting Cadence
Based on grid dates: Aug 23 to Sep 1, 2026 — roughly one post per day, alternating reels and carousels/photos, with carousels appearing every 2-4 days among daily reels.

## Language Patterns

### Tone Profile
Direct, conversational, second-person ("você"), confident/mentor voice. Mixes hard business language (tráfego pago, fechamento, escopo, ticket) with everyday, slightly provocative phrasing ("deu ruim," "cara triste," "duro"). Light use of emoji, mostly at caption end (👀, 👨🏻‍💻, 👇🏼).

### Hook Patterns
- **"You won't believe this happened" news hook** — Content 1 slide 1: states a surprising restriction/ban as fact, then promises an explanation ("Vem entender como isso prejudica ele →").
- **Numbered-benefit hook** — Content 2 slide 1: "5 COISAS QUE FAZEM SENTIDO AGORA QUE SOU RICO (mas quando eu era pobre, não faziam)" — contrast structure (before/after) plus a concrete visual proof (bank balance mockup).
- **Numbered-warning hook** — Content 3 slide 1: "8 FRASES QUE VOCÊ NÃO DEVERIA FALAR..." — a "don't do this" list format that promises to save the reader from a mistake.

### Call-to-Action Patterns
- Explicit follow CTA embedded as the carousel's own final slide (Content 1, slide 8): "Toda semana, eu compartilho estratégias... Me segue para aprender a aproveitar melhor as oportunidades desse mercado." This appears to be a recurring closing-slide template reused across carousels (the same slide image/text appeared as the profile-grid thumbnail for that post).
- Caption-only comment-bait CTA (Content 2): "Pelo que você trabalha pra ter? Comente aqui👇🏼" — a direct question, no follow ask.
- Implicit "save this" CTA (Content 3 caption): "Já salva pra não esquecer" — encourages the Instagram "save" action rather than follow/comment.

### Vocabulary Signature
Recurring words/phrases: "mercado digital," "tráfego pago," "estratégias," "crescer," "escopo," "fechamento," "investimento," "privilegiado," "duro" (broke/poor, slang), "cara triste" (sad face — negotiating tactic idiom), rhetorical mini-dialogues written as scripted exchanges (quote-style "X ... " turns used in Content 3 to show wrong phrasing).

### Style Notes
- Slides use plain, low-design meme/text-over-photo or text-over-plain-background style rather than polished branded templates — this reads as intentionally "native," screenshot-like content rather than agency-polished carousels.
- Numbered-list carousels sometimes break the "one slide = one list item" rule (Content 2 skips numbering visually on slide 2, and slide 5 is a silent video instead of a text slide), showing the format tolerates mixed media within one carousel.
- Long-form storytelling carousels (Content 1) use a slow-build structure — building tension for 4-5 slides before delivering the marketing lesson on slide 6-7 — rather than delivering the "point" up front.

### Engagement Drivers
- The **news-jacking post (Content 1)** has the lowest like count of the three but by far the highest comment count (318 vs 23-60) — political/current-events content clearly drives discussion/debate even when it earns fewer likes, likely because commenters take sides on the news story itself, not just the marketing lesson.
- The **wealth/freedom listicle (Content 2)** earned the most likes with a moderate comment count — relatable "freedom" content (autonomy, rest, saying no) appears to resonate broadly via likes/saves rather than debate.
- The **pure how-to sales listicle (Content 3)** got the fewest comments of the three despite solid likes — practical/educational "swipe file" content seems to be liked and saved more than discussed.

## Recommendations for Squad
1. **Standardize a closing "follow me" slide template** modeled on Content 1's slide 8 (short value promise + direct follow ask) and reuse it as the default final slide for Chroma Marketing's educational carousels — it is a proven, low-friction CTA pattern in this niche.
2. **Lead every carousel with a numbered or contrast-based hook** ("N coisas que...", "N coisas que fazem sentido agora que...") on slide 1 — this format consistently sets up clear list-based content and gives the reader an explicit reason to swipe.
3. **Alternate between two carousel archetypes**: (a) a "current-events/market story → marketing lesson" long-form narrative carousel for higher comment/discussion engagement, and (b) a "numbered practical listicle" carousel (mistakes to avoid, tips, phrases) for higher like/save engagement — planning both types keeps the content mix balanced for different KPIs.
4. **End listicle-style carousels with a caption CTA to save/comment**, not just follow — "Já salva pra não esquecer" and direct questions ("Pelo que você trabalha pra ter? Comente aqui") both performed well; Chroma Marketing carousels should pick one explicit action per post rather than defaulting only to "follow."
5. **Keep slide design minimal and native-feeling** (plain text-over-background or simple meme-style graphics rather than heavily branded templates) — this account's high engagement comes from carousels that read like organic screenshots/notes rather than polished ad creative, which may lower production cost while raising perceived authenticity for an educational-tone agency like Chroma Marketing.
